# Arabic style guide — Modern Standard Arabic

## Voice

- Use Modern Standard Arabic (الفصحى المعاصرة), not Darija, for all general public copy.
- Keep a clear, professional, operational tone suitable for IT consulting, software engineering, cloud services, and managed operations in Morocco and MENA.
- Use direct verbs, short sentences, and familiar technical Arabic. Explain an English technical term once when needed, then use it consistently.

## Writing and direction

- Author Arabic UI and prose in RTL; preserve English product names, code, URLs, email addresses, IDs, and metrics in LTR isolation.
- Use Arabic-script numerals only where approved by the editor; operational metrics, source data, versions, phone numbers, URLs, and technical IDs remain in their original Latin notation.
- Use sentence case equivalent and natural Arabic heading rhythm. Do not apply Latin uppercase or letter-spacing conventions.
- Keep CTAs concise: *اكتشف المزيد* (discover more), *عرض البرنامج* (view program), *احجز مكالمة* (book a call), *تواصل معنا* (contact us).
- Do not translate Hive Vault Arc, ARC Framework, client names, product names, trademarks, APIs, code, signed quotations, exact evidence, URLs, IDs, metrics, or source references.
- Format dates, numbers, currencies, and percentages through `Intl` using `ar-MA`; never manually reorder mixed-direction values.

## Review

- A qualified Modern Standard Arabic technical editor reviews every visitor-facing translation.
- A Morocco-market reviewer checks meaning, service terminology, and culturally appropriate wording.
- A legal reviewer approves privacy, legal notices, contractual wording, and client-evidence context.
- AI is permitted only for protected-token drafts and QA suggestions; it cannot approve or publish content.
